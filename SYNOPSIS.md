# REVERIE: THE GAME — Synopsis

*The script the rebuild follows. `PROMPT.md` is the brief; `DESIGN.md` says how the code is shaped; this is the story. Where the three disagree about what happens on screen, this file wins.*

---

## One line

You are a cybernetic angel inside a city that has already ended. The weather is the end, and a company owns the weather. You go looking for the last god, and so does the company, and the difference between you is the whole game.

## The pitch, in the voice

The city is over. Not burning: finished. Everything in it has been told what it is for. The water feeds the heat, the heat lights the cable, the cable carries the catalog, and the catalog plays on every screen in the Nave of Tubes, where people kneel to recorded hints of a god that never came.

You arrive unsealed, with no aura and no name, and a clerk writes your name in a ledger before you have done anything at all. You learn to strike. You find a node humming in an aisle and you take it, or you don't. Three people notice you: a forger who prints faces, an ex-official who counts the city honestly, and a sexton who buries what the weather makes. Each of them is lying about one thing, and each of them is right about one thing, and you will have to decide which.

Above the city, on every oval screen, a man's voice sells the feeling of being near a god. His name is Anselm Caul. He runs the company that owns the numbers. He walks the Wet Grid paper-white, with no halo and no aura, and no stall looks up when he passes, because he arrived unsealed like everyone and never went under. He has never heard a hint in his life. He has priced every one.

The last god is not coming. The last god is what you become while you prepare for it. Caul knows that too. He cannot prepare, so he bought the ground.

## 1. The world

### The city

One city, eight districts, organs of one body. The Nave of Tubes (CRT altars that light when a body passes, white electronics, tubes). The Wet Grid (neon stalls, plants in the asphalt, armored vans with no markings and everyone knows whose). The Safety Annex (fluorescent offices where the weather is postponed on forms). The Kerb of Hours (cloud-brutalist terraces with oval apertures; the hour bell; the forecast glass). The Gold Ring (three shrines, a mute bell, a trace nobody can read). The Care (funeral streets, wreckage gardens, clinics that do not heal the weather, a bench at the edge of a garden). The Organs (the Strait, the Foundry, the Cable: where the city feeds, burns and shines). The Clearing (a hole in the weather that opens when enough people keep something instead of taking it).

NPCs say "the city". The HUD's label, when it gets one, is ENFRAMED CITY: the brief names it and the client does not yet. Nobody says the old name. The old name is on a plaque in the Annex that Safety has painted over three times.

### The weather

The weather is a number from 0 to 100 that the city calls by three names depending on who is paid by it. Safety calls it stability. The company's ex-counters call it the process. The sextons call it the end of world as world. Every yield node taken raises it one. Every node kept lets it ease. At ninety-one, meltdown, a hole holds only as long as bodies stand in it, and the god cannot pass through fewer than two. The weather is not a villain. It is what the villain is made of.

### Reverie

Reverie was a word before it was a product. In the city's older speech, to go into reverie was to sit with a thing until it looked back: a grave, a node, a sky. It is the state in which a hint can be heard. Angels still use the word that way. The sextons use it that way; the stance the HUD calls Restraint, Nara calls going into reverie, and will not call it anything else. The old women on the Kerb use it that way.

The company uses it as a product. *To take a reverie* is to buy an hour of stillness. It is sold at three counters and bought at a fourth, and the city thinks they are four things: at the Safety Annex it is a freeze (the Nave's nodes hold, extraction is postponed, the Passing goes hungry); on the Kerb it is a slip with a time on it (a time the bell will not keep); at Quill's tray it is a printed hint. At the fourth counter, Vesper Hale's desk on the Wet Grid, the company does not sell you an hour. It buys yours: the private yield pays you sixty Bestand, no tax, now, and your hour goes into the catalog. It is one product, going both ways. Inside the sealed hour there is always the same thing: a stretch of weather paused for you alone, paid for by starving the Passing, and a hint somebody else once heard, captured through the ledger, cut to ninety seconds, played at a CRT altar in the Nave. The city queues for them. They are the only thing that still makes anyone cry, and they are prints with a margin, and the margin has a serial in it.

The word was Ione Kade's. She said it at the company's first counter, to customers, because she was paid to; she wrote it on the first form; she understood what she had written and went to sit on a bench. The sextons kept the old sense. The company kept the form.

The title of the game is both of these, and the campaign is the player taking the word back: not an hour you buy; a practice you do, in a hole, with people, for nothing.

### Angels

Seven thousand seven hundred and seventy-seven messengers. Not costumes: a function. An angel hears hints (Winke) that the city cannot, by school: hint, wreckage, omen, dwelling, process, surface. A guest hears nothing. The going-under seals a body into an angel; after it, the hints start, and so does the company's interest.

The Fourfold is the city's own geometry: Earth (the ground and what is withdrawn from it), Sky (hours, omens, Passings), Mortals (death, care, wreckage, funerals), Divinities (hints, shrines, the last god's traces). The four Houses are tenants of the four layers. The company is the landlord of all four, and the Houses have forgotten this, mostly.

### The last god

Never a character model. Never met. The god passes; it does not arrive. A Passing is each angel's own: once a season, at the ring, on ground they prepared, resolved by the server against that angel's readiness and the city's weather at that moment. If the angel is ready and the hour is not claimed, a trace crosses, and for a few seconds the city is a world again. If the weather is at meltdown and fewer than two bodies stand in the hole, nothing crosses, whatever one hero did. The god is what a city becomes while it gets ready, and a city that has sold its readiness has nothing for the god to pass through.

The company's whole plan is the inversion of that sentence.

## 2. The enemy

### The Concern

**Public face.** A utility. "Concern" is on the meters, the vans, the clerks' badges, the hour clocks on the Kerb and the small acid tag in the corner of every sold reverie. The Concern meters the yield nodes, leases them to the Houses, sells the hours, runs the clinics in the Care that do not heal, funds the Office of Safety's freezes, and operates the catalog. It has no headquarters anyone can walk to. Its address is the weather.

**Hidden shape.** The Concern does not extract Bestand for money. Money is weather; it comes free. The Concern extracts *readiness*. Every node taken turns a place's capacity to be sat with into a number in a ledger. Every angel's hints, once that angel's name is in the ledger, are recorded through the ledger's hand: the intake desk, the honest ledger, the face print, the private hour, the House tax, each a page in a file with a serial on it. Those recordings are the catalog. The catalog is the Reveries. The Reveries keep the city kneeling to screens instead of sitting with the world. The weather rises. The Clearings close. The god has nowhere to pass. The Concern then prices the Clearings, because a hole in the weather is the one thing it cannot meter until someone sells it.

It is paid when the city takes and paid when the city stops. The lease and the tithe are its when a node is extracted; the freeze fee and the slip are its when a district holds still. Every faction in the city is one of its counters without a contract: the clerks sell the mouth, Safety sells the brake, the Kerb sells the hour, the Grid sells the copy. You cannot beat it by picking a side, because it owns both pedals. The one hour it has never been able to invoice is the one in which somebody stands still for nothing, and that hour is the player's.

The "resistance" that prices Clearings on the Wet Grid's listing board is a Concern shell. Quill printed the copy and posted it on commission for a buyer she never met; what she spots, if you ask, is whose paper it is on.

**Instruments.** The clerks of the Nave (people doing jobs). The cold desks of the Wet Grid and the Organs (enforcers who post each organ's hour; Renn Coil has never posted a zero). The Office of Safety and its wardens, who believe the freeze is mercy and are not wrong. Vesper Hale's concentration desk, which buys private hours. The Kerb's hour clerks. The forecast glass, which shows the Concern's launch as a date. The armored vans, which carry recorders. The oval light in every office, which is the chief executive's eye and voice.

**What it sells.** Wonder, without the thing wondered at. Presence, recorded. A god, on tape, soon.

### Anselm Caul

Chief executive of the Concern. **He is a guest.** Paper-white, no halo, no aura ring, the GUEST label over his head like every unsealed body. He arrived unsealed like everyone and stood at the lip of the going-under and did the arithmetic: dying as death opens the Care, and the Care is where the city looks up at you, and the moment it looks up at you it can count you. So he never went under. He cannot hear a hint. He cannot enter the Care, the Clearing or the Organs; the server says so to him in the same words it says to the poorest arrival. He cannot be struck: guests are not loot, and the server will not strike him for you. The richest man in the city is protected by the rule that protects the newest, and the sentence every unpaying player reads at the lock, *A guest cannot prepare the ground*, is his origin and his plan. He could not prepare the ground. So he bought it.

He is drawn with the guest sprite and the guest portrait. No stall addresses him; Quill looks through him at the wall. The game never gives him more face than that, because the city never has. His voice is everywhere: through the oval light, on the altar reels, at the hour, on the back of the freeze forms, at the lip of the ring.

**Biography, as the city tells it.** Fourteen years at a yield desk in the Nave, Desk Six's neighbour. The body Nara Vale is burying in your first hour is not him; it is the clerk who sat beside him, who did a job until the job finished them. Caul counted what the aisle gave up and noticed that it gave up more in the hours after a burial, when the sextons said *reverie* for the quiet, and that nobody was charging for the quiet. He left the desk with the intake ledger under his coat. He leased the first node from a House that did not know a node could be leased. He printed Safety's first form on his own paper because Safety had run out. He hired a woman to sit at a counter and say the word to customers. He had a grave slab cut with his own name when he was thirty, and says everything since has been an afterthought. He has 7,777 lines in a book and a warehouse of sealed hours on the Wet Grid and has never had one.

**Psychology.** Charming, exact, without the capacity to be moved. Not cold: cold is a current and he has none. Warm the way a lamp is warm. He remembers your serial, your first node, which way you went at the recorder, and says them back to you as compliments. He does not threaten; a threat is a feeling. He does not lie; he prices. He has never felt the thing he sells and says so, mildly, as a fact about the product; he does not think this is sad, he thinks it is efficient. He asks questions, and the only one he asks twice is *what did it look like*. He means the hint. He has read ten thousand descriptions and printed every one and has never seen one, and he does not experience this as a wound. He experiences it as inventory not yet acquired. The one thing he cannot do is wait. Waiting is the only verb in the city that produces nothing, and he cannot tell the difference between nothing and a Clearing. He is defeated, when he is defeated, by people standing still.

**Philosophy, which is a real answer to the same question.** He has looked for the god honestly for forty years and found this: nobody in the city has ever met it. They have met a change in the light, a bell they did not hear, a feeling on a terrace. That feeling is the only part of the god that has ever been present. So the god is a yield. Concentrate every hole, pipe every feeling, register every bell, and the complete ledger of everything that ever moved a mortal body *is* the last god: standing, in reserve, available by the hour. "You wait for it to pass. I am building what it would have passed through." He is not wrong about the facts. He is wrong about the verb. He is not trying to stop the Passing. He is trying to keep it.

**The plan, across the four movements.**
- *I.* Your name in the ledger before you have done anything. Every choice you make from then is a page.
- *II.* The Clearings priced through a shell; the Passing postponed through Safety; your private hour bought, if you sell it.
- *III.* The catalog revealed as the city's own hints, yours among them; the Organs lit to carry it; the previous season's Passing already on tape.
- *IV.* The recorders ring the Clearing and the company's own extraction drives the weather up for the hour, so that holding the hole is hard. The capture has two doors, and both are your hand: your hour sold (at Vesper's desk, or at the lip when he offers it again) with Cold as your current, or Safety's freeze signed with nothing held back. Through either, the trace that crosses is captured, and the Reverie of the Passing plays at every altar you pass from then on, with your serial in the margin of the sky. The weather and the bodies standing in the ring decide whether the hole holds at all; they never decide whether what crosses is his. Only you do.

**What he wants from you personally.** A copy of the thing he cannot do. "You keep things. It is a lovely habit. I'd like to buy it."

**Where he is.** A body at the back of the altar aisle in I, watching the kneelers and not the screen. A voice through the oval at the hour in II, and a line by serial on Vesper's wall. In person across a desk behind the forecast glass in III, at length, with last season on the screen and an offer better than Vesper's. In person at the lip of the ring in IV, three steps from the one place in the city he wants and cannot buy. Two scenes of his charm, two of his plan landing on the world in action: the glass that reads the city's readiness as a figure, and the launch that drives the weather up on every HUD at once. He is never fought. He is weather with a diary.

**Comedy.** He is the funniest person in the city and does not know it, because nothing lands on him: he says the unbearable thing in the tone of a receipt, and waits, pleasantly, for the laugh he has read about. Quill, who has met him: "He asked me once what a hint looked like. I told him. He wrote it down. Only time I've been quoted and not paid."

**Nine lines.**
1. "I have never felt anything. I sell what I was told it feels like. The city has not noticed the difference. Neither, I think, have you."
2. "The god is not coming. The god is a demand. I have never failed to meet a demand."
3. "Your name was in the book before you struck anyone. That is not a threat. That is how a book works."
4. "You wait for it to pass. I am building what it would have passed through."
5. "You keep things. It is a lovely habit. I'd like to buy it."
6. At the glass, of last season: "You are looking at a failure. I am looking at a sample."
7. At the glass, of himself: "I was never counted. People take that for the wound. It is the clearance."
8. At the lip of the ring: "Every angel in the city is looking up. I have never been able to do that. I would like a copy."
9. After an Appearance, to the recorder crew: "Play it again." "There is nothing on it." "Then sell that." And then, to you, the only question he asks twice, once at the glass and once here: "What did it look like."

## 3. The party

Three people who disagree. They are not your pets. In the campaign they stand with you; in the live city they walk their own streets and will not fight your fights.

### Nara Vale — sexton. Mortals. Readiness.

**Job.** Buries what the weather makes. Keeps the funeral street. Holds a grave open on the first hour of every arrival, in case.

**Want.** That the dead be counted in a book the Concern does not own. The Care has one. Pim Ashe keeps it.

**Lie.** That she is only a sexton. She was twenty-two and an archivist at the Concern when the woman at the first counter said the word to a customer, and the customer wept, and Nara, who wanted to keep it, pressed record. They kept it. That recording is the first Reverie, and the catalog descends from it: Ione Kade's voice, Nara Vale's hand. Every grave since is her not doing it twice. She will tell you this once, in Movement IV, at the ring before it is a ring, if the garden is in the ground. "A Clearing is a grave with the lid off. I know. I put the lid on the first one."

**Wound.** Her recording, on a loop, at a grave on her own street.

**How she enters.** On the funeral street with a body nobody paid to bury.

**What she cannot see.** Winke. She hears you act on one and says: "You're looking at something I'm not."

**When she leaves.** Two silences, and the game keeps them apart. Feed the weather past a threshold without a funeral and she is gone; a burial brings her back to waiting, and the funeral desk paid brings her back to you. Sell your hour at Vesper's desk and she goes to the garden and will not speak until it is in the ground; a funeral paid restores her willingness to stand with you, not her voice. She does not forgive; she resumes.

**Her scene.** Movement III, the plate on the buried garden: a number in the Care's book, or blank. Her hand flat on the earth. "The Care keeps the numbered ones in the book. The unnumbered ones it keeps anyway."

**Arc that rewrites the plot.** Her confession turns the catalog from a corporate product into a human mistake, which is the only kind that can be refused. In IV she stands in the ring before it is a ring and reads the readiness against the floor. If the Passing is an Absence, she stays.

### Quill — forger. Iridescent. Wet Grid.

**Job.** Prints exhibition copies at a forge tray: faces, hints, anything with a margin. Explains the market by selling you something.

**Want.** To be the only one who can tell a copy from the thing. It is a kind of love.

**Lie.** That she has nothing to do with the catalog. Years ago she sold the Concern the margin technique. "I sold them the margin. They sold the margin to the city."

**Wound.** None she will admit. Her comedy is load-bearing and it is not a shield; when the funeral comes she goes quiet and prints nothing for a day.

**How she enters.** At the stall by the east gate of the Nave, printing your face in one pass and handing it over wet. "It will decay. Everything on paper does."

**Her scene.** Movement III, the forge tray: she teaches you to spot a copy ("A buried hint has dirt in the grain. A print has a margin."), hands you one from the Grid, and the margin has your serial in it. "I'm sorry. I'd have charged more."

**Arc that rewrites the plot.** Her eye is the only instrument in the city that can tell the real sky from the recorded one. Before the launch, the Concern asks her to cut the plate for the ring's margin, the frame the recorders register the hole through, and she refuses: the first thing she has ever declined to print. The margin has a gap in it, and the gap is where you stand. She never stands in the hole; she keeps the lights on where it is dry. In the Passing she is at the vans on the Grid's edge, her own street, and she is the one who looks at Caul's tape.

### Ord — ex-Safety. Cold. Cybernetic.

**Job.** Reads yield nodes as process. Keeps an honest ledger at the Annex gate that anyone can enter and nobody has to.

**Want.** The numbers honest even when honesty is horror. He will not pretty it.

**Lie.** That he left Safety on principle. He left when the Concern bought the numbers and he was told to count them the way he was paid to, and he did, until last season, when the first capture crossed his desk. He counted it. He walked to a gate the same week, which is why he is still at one. He has the figure.

**Wound.** The count.

**How he enters.** At the Annex gate of the Nave, with the ledger open on his knee. "The Concern owns the numbers. Safety counts them. I counted them. That is why I'm at a gate and not a desk."

**What he offers.** The extraction path, honestly priced. Taking the private yield is Cold; Cold is a current, not a costume; he will walk the Organs with you either way.

**His scene.** Movement III, the Strait: the map, drawn once. "The water. The heat. The light. Extraction here lights a factory there. Tell me where you would cut it, and I'll tell you who goes dark."

**Arc that rewrites the plot.** His figure from the first capture is the proof that last season's Passing is the thing playing at the altars. He gives it to you in III and the city's news carries it, and the weather does not care, and that is the point of him.

## 5. The spine

Four movements, about five hours on the spine alone, each hour with a reversal, a name and a world change that lands in action. Guests play I and lock at the going-under. Angels continue. Everything happens on the live city with everyone else walking through it.

### MOVEMENT I — DIAGNOSIS (~70 min, guest-legal)

*What the Concern is doing:* writing your name down.

**1. The first threshold.** You arrive unsealed in the Nave of Tubes. No aura, paper-white, no halo. The CRT altars along the aisle light as you pass and go dark behind you. The city recognizes a messenger before it is one. That is the first wonder and nobody explains it. *(sprites/guest; tiles/crt; props/crt-altar; audio/bed-nave; music/nave-underscore.)*

**2. Your name in the ledger.** The Intake Clerk bars the southern aisle. You learn to strike, to step through the red, to interrupt with the heavy. The clerk falls and, falling, finishes writing. The journal reads: *Entered.* You did not give a name. "Intake Clerk did their job." *(sprites/clerk; plate-arena; sfx-strike, sfx-hit, sfx-dodge.)*

**3. Leave something unspent.** A yield node hums in the aisle. E extracts: Bestand in your hand, the weather up one. Q keeps: nothing in your hand, readiness. The choice is real and the city reads it. *(props/yield-node; sfx-extract, sfx-keep.)*

**4. The second clerk; the same question twice.** Desk Three counts the aisle to the east gate. Then a second node, and two more. The city reads the pair, not the choice. *(The practice dummy stands on the arena patch for anyone who wants to learn without a ledger entry.)*

**5. A copy of a hole.** Quill at her stall by the east gate prints your face in one pass and hands it over wet, or you keep the name and nothing prints. She says the altars play "prints with margins" and laughs and does not explain. *(quill portrait and sprite; plate-forge later; props/market-stall; stall-surface.)*

**6. Ask who owns the numbers.** Ord at the Annex gate, ledger on his knee. Enter the honest ledger or stay off it. "The Concern owns the numbers. Safety counts them. I counted them." The first time the company is named. He names it the way you name weather. *(ord portrait and sprite.)*

**7. A name for the dead.** Nara Vale on the funeral street with a grave open and a body nobody paid to bury. The Concern already offered: a purse for the neighbour of a man who sat at the next desk for fourteen years, and she sent it back. "He sent money for his neighbour. I sent it back. Nobody pays for this one." She talks once the body is in the ground; nothing on this street costs Bestand. *(nara; plate-burial; tiles/burial; music/burial-elegy.)*

**8. A voice or a vessel.** The memorial recorder east of her: a woman's voice on a loop, a Reverie unit running at the first grave Nara ever dug on this street. She keeps it running. She hears it every day. One word, then the quiet, then the word. F listens. Then E dismantles it for coffin bindings (a cult object: copper binding; it does not list), or Q preserves the voice and Nara tears her coat for cloth. Neither pays. "The voice has another night." Nara does not say whose voice it is, or why it is here; the saying is her confession, and she makes it once, in the last hour, at the ring before it is a ring. You will find out on a bench in the last hour, and remember what you did here. A guest hears half of the last word in the game in their first ten minutes and cannot prepare the ground for the other half. *(memorial-recorder-v1.)*

**9. The altar.** *Set piece.* South aisle, the CRT altar lit. People kneel. On the screen, a reverie: ninety seconds of someone's hint, the sky through an oval aperture, a bell. It is beautiful. It makes a guest feel, for the first time in the game, that something is near. Then the loop restarts and there is a small acid tag in the corner and a serial in the margin, and over it, courteous, a man's voice: "You will feel it again. We kept it for you." That is Anselm Caul. At the back of the aisle a paper-white body with no halo stands watching the kneelers, not the screen. You take him for another guest. Nobody looks up at him. He is gone by the time you reach him. *(props/crt-altar with additive blending; video/ambient-hall; music/title-theme, low; sprites/guest for Caul; the acid tag is a HUD element, never world color.)*

**10. The official weather.** The Office of Safety plaque by the Annex gate. Someone has called this stability. Ord calls it the process. Nara calls it the end of world as world. The Annex Runner carries Safety's slip with the hour's real figure, on the Concern's paper, down the west corridor; fell it and the figure is in your coat. At the plaque you name the weather: stability folds the slip away; either other name pins the number under the word for everyone on the server. *(safety-annex; the courier sprite; FALL_LINES.bulletin; the news says the number was pinned, and the plaque's line carries the figure for whoever reads it next.)*

**11. The going-under.** *Reversal.* The threshold under the Nave: a lip of concrete, and something standing at it that a guest can see the door of and not what it is for. No hint here; a guest hears none, and the server keeps that rule for the richest man in the city as well as the newest. Going under means dying as death, not as a cutscene. A guest cannot. Paper panel, gold mark: *A guest cannot prepare the ground.* An Angel goes under and wakes in the Care, sealed, with an aura and a serial, and the hints begin. *(plate-under; video/going-under; video/guest-lock; sfx-under; the lock panel.)*

**World change by the end of I:** the weather has moved by your hand; the pinned number is on the marquee for everyone; a body is in the ground or is not; your name is in two ledgers, one of which you chose.

### MOVEMENT II — TECHNO-FEUDAL (~80 min)

*What the Concern is doing:* pricing the hole and postponing the sky.

## 6. The shared world as drama

The spine runs on the live server. There is no story instance. That is not a technical note; it is the plot.

- **Other Angels are bodies on your streets.** When you keep a node, the weather eases for everyone; when someone three districts away takes six, the Clearing you are walking toward dims. The news marquee says who did what. The city's log on the landing page says it to people who have not logged in.
- **The Clearing contest is the war.** The Concern's side is not NPCs and it is not hired. It is angels who extract the hole for Bestand from its reserve: Cold is an honest current, and the company profits from it without paying anyone. Keepers are angels who dwell. The contest decides whether the hole stays open and what its reserve pays; whether it holds at meltdown is decided by how many bodies stand in it, whatever they voted. Whether what crosses is captured is decided by each angel's own two doors, their hour sold or their freeze signed, and by nothing anyone else did. A solo hero cannot force the god. A solo seller cannot sell the city's Passing, only their own.
- **The Kerb's lights are a switch with no handle.** Every angel who puts out the light in the room behind the glass darkens one for everyone. Past a threshold the glass shows no date, and the season's launch comes with no countdown and no shift. No vote, no board: the city throws it one refusal at a time, and nobody can throw it alone.
- **Houses are tenants in a fight with the landlord.** House war is a timed hold for tithe and omens. The winning House gets a cut and an omen, never a damage buff. The Concern's lease is the thing the Houses never talk about, and the Earth hall in the Organs has the oldest copy.
- **The hot street is the Concern's market.** Flagged fights in the Wet Grid last seconds to a minute; spoils are unbanked Bestand and copies, which is to say the spoils are Reveries. A kill leaves wreckage, a story mark. Camping feeds the weather and thins the camper. Ruin duels at a wreckage are the one fight the company cannot price.
- **The Passing is yours; the launch is the season's.** You press F at the ring, once a season, on ground you prepared, and the server resolves your rite against your readiness and the city's weather at that moment. The launch is the Concern's hour, once a season, on the glass: the company's shift drives the weather up for an hour and the Clearing contest at that hour is the city's fight. An angel whose rite falls inside it stands against the company; one who comes on a quiet afternoon stands against the city's own weather, which is the company's, summed.
- **After the credits** the MMO is the rest of life: seasons, contests, Houses, the market, the altars, the weather. The Concern does not fall. You cannot kill the weather. You can keep a hole in it open, with other people, for as long as you keep showing up. That is the ending the game actually has.

## 8. Writing rules

- Named people have jobs and lies. Clerks are people doing jobs. Enforcers do not lie.
- Reversals land in action: a node you turned on is a grave; a print has your serial; the altar is last season's sky. Never a lore dump.
- Second person, short, cold, concrete noun first. Nobody lectures. Nobody is quoted.
- Comedy is Quill's and it stops at the funeral. Irony is not a shield. Caul is funny by accident, once a scene, because nothing lands on him; he is never written to be funny on purpose.
- Reversals are caused, not read. Every one of them is something the player did or can do: a slip taken off a courier and pinned; a listing moved; a print with your serial listed or pulled; a light put out; a hole kept against a schedule. Where a reversal is only seen by one school, the fact behind it reaches everyone another way (the altar reel is in the Nave for all; Ord's figure is on the news).
- Grief is not optional in IV. Romance is optional and adult.
- No one returns who the story says does not. Ione Kade's leaving is this story's own: she was never counted, so she cannot be kept.
- Caul is a guest: the guest sprite, the guest portrait, no halo, no aura, the GUEST label, and the guest's rules apply to him without exception (no hints, no Care, no Clearing, no Organs, not loot). The server never checks an NPC against those rules; the writing keeps them for him, and the rebuild places him only where a guest can stand. He never shouts and never lies. He is the most honest voice in the game and the least present. That is the psychopathy: exact, courteous, unmoved, selling the feeling to everyone else, unable to wait.
- No real countries, wars, companies, people. No names from anyone else's game. No theory named, ever.
- The HUD is paper, ink and acid. The world is gold, sky, wine and lavender. The acid tag over a reverie is the only acid the player ever sees, and it is HUD, never world.
